**Option A**

Nobody hands a 10-year-old the car keys.

First you watch. Then you practise with an adult beside you. Then you get a learner's permit. Only after tests do you get a full licence. And if you drive badly, you can lose it.

AI helpers should be treated the same way. I call it: autonomy is earned.

🔹 Level 0: it only watches
🔹 Level 1: it gives advice
🔹 Level 2: it gets things ready, and a person checks
🔹 Level 3: it does small jobs, only with a signed OK
🔹 Level 4: it does set jobs alone, inside hard limits

To move up, it must pass four tests, one after another. The quick ones go first, the slow, costly one goes last. Then a gate decides. If even one critical test fails, the answer is no, however good the other marks are.

The video shows a made-up expense helper moving up, then stepping back down when an update breaks one test.

One more thing I find humbling: 50 perfect tests in a row only tell us the helper probably fails fewer than 6 times in 100. Tests give evidence, not proof.

The full guide, with a plain-words glossary and the technical details underneath: https://keshavaai.in/journal/autonomy-is-earned?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=post

**Option B (short)**

A helper that gets 49 out of 50 tests right can still be stopped by the gate.

Why? One of the tests was critical, and it failed. Good marks elsewhere cannot hide that. Like a car that is fast but has no brakes.

That is "autonomy is earned": freedom in steps, proved by tests, and taken back if a critical test fails. 42-second animation and the full guide: https://keshavaai.in/journal/autonomy-is-earned?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=post
